## 2. Hackathon prototype -> [`hackathon/`](hackathon/)

### The Silent Garden

**Resit team:** Rayna Jong and Slyman Arnaot  
**Tool:** ComfyUI  
**SDG:** 13 – Climate Action

### Problem and intended audience

Heat and extreme weather can affect when plants flower and how much food is available for pollinators. This can disrupt the relationships between plants and insects that help ecosystems function. [European Environment Agency desk research source](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/protecting-and-restoring-europes-wild-pollinators-and-their-habitats)

Our target audience is young adults aged 18–30 who watch short-form digital content. A typical viewer is a university student scrolling through a feed who has heard about climate change and has little background in pollination. They need a quick, concrete explanation of why extreme heat matters to a garden and the insects that rely on it. Teachers and environmental organisations can share the film. The intended audience excludes ecology specialists and viewers looking for detailed conservation guidance.

| Intended viewer need | How the film responds |
| --- | --- |
| Understand the connection without knowing ecology terms | The same garden goes from flowering and active to hot and quiet. Viewers can follow the change through what happens to the flowers and insects. |
| Follow the story during a brief viewing | The film follows one location in around 30 seconds, with narration and a short closing line. |
| Have a concrete image to remember | A single surviving flower and the final line, “When nature becomes silent... we should listen,” bring the story back to the garden. |

These are design assumptions based on the desk research and the intended viewing context.

### What we built

The Silent Garden is a short AI-generated climate awareness film. Its goal is to help viewers connect climate stress to a visible change in a familiar ecosystem. [Watch The Silent Garden](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pmWtrWT7KSM)

### Original scenario

A garden is full of flowers, bees and butterflies. As temperatures rise with climate change, the flowers dry up and disappear. With less food and habitat, the bees disappear, followed by the butterflies. The once-lively garden falls silent. One flower survives.

A short fact card could communicate the basic link. The film lets viewers follow the same garden over time and hear the narration as the signs of life disappear. That change is the part we wanted viewers to remember after a brief viewing.

### ComfyUI workflow

Viewers open the finished film. The production inputs are our scene prompts, selected still images, motion prompts, timed narration script and intermediate media files. The JSON exports in the [hackathon folder](hackathon/) store the ComfyUI graphs and settings. Open each graph in ComfyUI, install any missing nodes and model files, and reselect the named input media in the loader nodes. Run each graph separately and pass its saved output to the next stage as needed.

1. **Generate the garden stills.** The exports `Silent_Garden_Shot_01.json` through `Silent_Garden_Shot_08.json` use Z-Image Turbo (`z_image_turbo_bf16.safetensors`), the Qwen 3 4B text encoder and `ae.safetensors` for VAE decoding. Positive prompts describe each stage of the garden and negative prompts describe visual artifacts to avoid. The graph uses a 1024 × 1024 latent, AuraFlow shift 3, and a KSampler with 9 steps, CFG 1, Euler, simple scheduling and denoise 1. The sampler is set to randomize its seed. VAE Decode and Save Image turn the sampled latent into a saved still.
2. **Animate the stills.** In `Silent_Garden_Video_Shot_04.json`, Load Image passes `ComfyUI_00004_.png` into the Wan 2.2 image-to-video subgraph. The prompt asks for slow upward camera movement, a hot breeze and subtle heat haze. In `Silent_Garden_Video_CloseUp.json`, the input is `ComfyUI_00011_.png` and the prompt asks to hold the image nearly still. Both use 640 × 640 output, Wan 2.2 14B FP8 high- and low-noise models with their LightX2V LoRAs, UMT5 XXL and the Wan 2.1 VAE. Save Video writes the generated clip. These two exports document examples of the motion stage.
3. **Build the closing card.** `Silent_Garden_Shot_09_Ending.json` passes a 640 × 640 black image through Text Overlay and Save Image. The overlay uses white, centered text at size 48 for the closing message.
4. **Append the last clip.** In `Merging last clip.json`, Load Video connects the assembled `Silent_Garden_Final_00001_.mp4` to Concatenate Video’s `video0` and `Wan2.2_i2v_00010_.mp4` to `video1`. Save Video writes the result. The earlier sequence enters this graph as an assembled MP4, so the export documents the final append.
5. **Generate narration.** In `Audio Generating.json`, the Chatterbox engine is set to v3 and English. It feeds Unified TTS SRT, which contains the narration script with timestamps. Save Audio Advanced exports the generated track as FLAC.
6. **Combine picture and sound.** In `AUDIO + VIDEO MERGING.json`, Load Video sends `ComfyUI_00002_.mp4` to Concatenate Video’s `video0`. Load Audio sends `ComfyUI_00019.flac` to `complete_audio`. The `video1` port is empty. Save Video takes the combined output, with format and codec set to auto.
7. **Resize the video.** In `Upscaling.json`, Load Video reads `ComfyUI_00007_.mp4`. Get Video Components splits it into frames, audio and frame rate. Image Scale resizes the frames to 1280 × 720 using bicubic interpolation with cropping disabled. Create Video receives the resized frames along with the source audio and frame rate, then passes the result to Save Video.

The finished film demonstrates the output of this production process. The exports show the individual stages and their file handoffs. The complete earlier clip sequence is already assembled at the input of the final-clip merge.

### Ethical reflection

The garden is fictional and the visuals are AI-generated. The story uses silence as a metaphor for ecological loss. A viewer could mistake realistic AI footage for a recording of a real event or assume that all gardens respond to heat in the same way. The project description identifies the setting as fictional and explains the limits of the example. Climate change is one pressure on pollinators alongside factors such as habitat loss and pesticides. Generating and regenerating media also uses energy, so the educational value of each extra attempt matters.

## 4. Reflection

**What is the most important thing I learned this week?**
The most important thing I learned was how much planning is needed when using AI to create a complete project. It is not just about generating an image or video. We had to think about the story, prompts, consistency between scenes, movement, narration, timing and the final message.
I also learned that AI-generated content can be useful for communication, but we still need to think about whether what we create could mislead people.
**Where does this connect to "AI for Good"?**
Our project connects to AI for Good because we used AI as a tool for environmental awareness rather than just creating something for entertainment. Existing solutions already focus on things like habitat restoration, reducing pesticides and monitoring pollinators. Our contribution was different: we focused on communication and awareness, using AI to make the issue understandable and emotionally meaningful for a young audience.
