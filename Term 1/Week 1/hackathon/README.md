**Project title:** 
Campus Dutch

**My pair partner:** 
Felix Reichle

**Tool we had to use:** 
Bolt.new

**SDG we had to address:**
SDG 4: Quality Education

**What problem does it solve, and for whom?**
Campus Dutch is made for international students in the Netherlands with little or basic knowledge of Dutch. While apps like Duolingo teach general language for everyday situations, Campus Dutch focuses on university terms and instructions. Students can often use English in daily conversations, but Dutch words related to grades, deadlines, schedules and assignments may still be confusing.

**What did you build?**
We built a Dutch vocabulary learning app with short lessons, flashcards and multiple-choice quizzes. Users can study university terms, receive simple English explanations, retry incorrect answers, earn badges and track their progress. The app also includes a Word of the Day, daily reviews, Demo Mode and a language menu showing languages we would like to support in the future.

**Link to the live thing (if any):**
https://campus-dutch-learnin-gf93.bolt.host 

**How do I run it?**
Open the live link in a web browser and click Start Learning. Select your preferred language and begin a lesson. No account or download is needed, and the user’s learning progress and language preference are saved automatically on their device.

**Who did what?**
**Together:** We brainstormed ideas for international students, chose the Campus Dutch concept and discussed the target group, features and privacy. We decided not to require profiles and to save progress locally. We also worked together on the main Bolt prompts and used ChatGPT to make them clearer and avoid wasting Bolt tokens.

**Rayna:** I collected feedback from an international student, reviewed the slides and gave Felix feedback. I was the project owner and I made the final Bolt changes, including the Word of the Day feature, language selector, dashboard improvements, removal of unnecessary sections and duplicate buttons, and improvements to the quiz navigation. I also worked on the Today’s Review issue before Felix continued fixing it. I prepared the introduction for the presentation and completed my own README and submission.

**Felix:** Felix fixed several earlier issues in the app using follow-up prompts. He created the presentation slides and updated them after receiving my feedback. He also continued working on the Today’s Review date issue when it was not updating correctly. Felix will present the solution, ethical reflection and live demonstration. He also completed his own README and submission.

**Ethical reflection - what are the risks of your tool? Who could it harm?**
Campus Dutch supports multiple languages, but it may still exclude students whose preferred language is unavailable. It also assumes that users have access to a suitable device and the internet. Incorrect or oversimplified translations could confuse students about important deadlines, grades or assignments, especially if meanings differ between languages. The translations should therefore be checked carefully. The app is meant to support learning and should not replace official university information or direct communication with a lecturer.

## 4. Reflection

**What is the most important thing I learned this week?**
The most important thing I learned this week is that AI can create a working app from a written prompt, but the result depends on how clearly and specifically the prompt is written. We still had to make decisions about the target group, content, design and features, and test whether everything worked correctly. I learned that AI can make the building process faster, but it does not replace our own ideas, decisions and checks.

**Where does this connect to "AI for Good"?**
Campus Dutch connects to AI for Good because it uses AI to create a learning tool that can make education more accessible for international students. Understanding Dutch university terms can help students follow instructions, communicate with lecturers and feel more confident while studying in the Netherlands. However, translations can be incorrect or too simple, so the app should support official university information rather than replace it.
